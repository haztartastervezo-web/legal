5. Nutrition calculations, data sources and limitations

Important: energy, macro-, micronutrient, vitamin and mineral values shown in the app, including percentages of reference values, are estimates provided solely for general information and interest. They are not medical, dietetic, diagnostic, treatment or personalised nutrition advice.

Nutrient data source: ingredient nutrient data is based on the U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) FoodData Central database. FoodData Central combines multiple types of data, including laboratory analytical data, calculated and published-literature data, and label data for some commercial products. Values in the database therefore are not a laboratory measurement of the exact food you actually consume. Real nutrient content can vary with variety, brand, growing conditions, ripeness, processing, preparation, storage and other factors; source data may also be incomplete, outdated, inaccurate or erroneous.

USDA source: U.S. Department of Agriculture, Agricultural Research Service, Beltsville Human Nutrition Research Center, FoodData Central.

EU reference values: for percentages of daily reference values, the app uses the adult Reference Intakes (RI) and vitamin/mineral Nutrient Reference Values (NRV) set out in Annex XIII of Regulation (EU) No 1169/2011. These are general food-labelling reference values, not an individual's personal nutritional requirement and not a personalised recommended daily allowance.

How calculations work: the app uses straightforward arithmetic proportional scaling and addition based on the selected ingredient's FoodData Central values, the quantities entered in the recipe and the stated number of servings. Results are displayed per serving; a percentage of a reference value is the mathematical ratio between the calculated per-serving amount and the applicable EU RI/NRV. The app does not perform laboratory analysis, bioavailability modelling, absorption correction, health assessment or personalised requirement calculation.

Inaccuracies may arise from an incorrect ingredient match, differences between raw and cooked foods, imprecise quantities or units, missing database values, rounding, an incorrect serving count, data-entry mistakes or errors in source data. Every displayed result should therefore be treated as an estimate that may contain errors.

If you have a food allergy, intolerance, medical condition, pregnancy, medication, are planning nutrition for a child, suspect a deficiency, or have a special dietary requirement, do not base healthcare decisions on these calculations. Check the actual product labels and seek advice from an appropriate healthcare professional where needed. Free-text notes in the app are likewise not a substitute for medical records or emergency care.
