Movement and Everyday Activity Safety Notice

Last updated: October 19, 2026

Routine Cards & Moves offers general movement ideas and visual prompts for everyday family activities. An adult should choose, explain and supervise activities according to the child's age, abilities and circumstances.

Before starting

  • Use a clear space with a stable, non-slip surface. Keep away from stairs, traffic, sharp edges and unstable furniture.
  • Choose gentle movements that the participant can perform comfortably. Do not force a position or movement, and do not use competitive timing to override comfort.
  • Seek advice from an appropriately qualified professional when a medical condition, injury, recent surgery, balance difficulty or other concern could affect safe participation.
  • Check animations and written instructions together. An illustration is not a personalised assessment; skip a movement if the technique is unclear.

During an activity

Allow breaks and normal breathing. Stop for pain, dizziness, nausea, unusual breathlessness, faintness or distress. Do not restrain a child, force physical contact, apply pressure to the neck or chest, or require breath holding. Partner activities and any pressure-based movement require willing participation and appropriate adult judgement. Seek medical help where symptoms warrant it; in an emergency, contact local emergency services.

Everyday task cards

A task card is a prompt, not an instruction that the child is ready to perform it alone. Medication and vitamins must be selected, dosed, stored and supervised by a responsible adult according to professional instructions. A card or reminder does not verify a dose or confirm that medicine was taken. Cooking, cleaning, bathing, animal care and outdoor tasks may also require direct supervision. Keep medicines, cleaning chemicals, heat sources and sharp tools out of inappropriate reach.

Purpose and limits

The app does not provide diagnosis, treatment, rehabilitation or emergency monitoring. “Calming”, “focus” and similar descriptions do not promise a clinical or neurological effect. Responses vary. Movement breaks should support the child's comfort and everyday participation. They should never replace indicated care or be used as punishment.

Local reminders can be delayed or missed. Do not rely on them for medication timing, safeguarding or another critical alert. Read the Terms of Use for service limits and your statutory rights.
